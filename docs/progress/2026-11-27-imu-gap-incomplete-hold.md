# IMU gap policy: the hold check on INCOMPLETE frames ([#8](https://github.com/atinfinity/glim_localization/issues/8))

- **Change**: the first two "known limits that can still publish a pose" of #8.
  - A frame whose scan end the IMU queue does not reach (`INCOMPLETE`), outside a gap episode, now gets the hold check on upstream's real queue over (last pose, frame]. A hold of at most `max_imu_gap` proceeds, as before: this is the normal `end_of_sequence` flush. A longer hold drops the frame without a pose, counted in `incomplete_drops()`. A hold above `imu_gap_reset_max` goes LOST. Before, such a frame always proceeded and upstream held the last sample across the whole tail (up to 0.2 s in the seeded streams).
  - `handle_imu_gap` returns a verdict with three values instead of a bool: proceed, drop (the IMU window restarts at the frame, as for a gap episode or LOST), or drop and keep the IMU data. The `INCOMPLETE` drop keeps it: the last pose stays the reference, so the queue and the gap history must keep everything since that pose. Trimming them, as every other drop does, made the next frame miss a hole before the dropped frame in the history while the queue still held it, and the hold check then went LOST as an internal disagreement.
  - The 5th non-finite IMU stamp in a row now goes LOST when there is a pose. Before, the counter was only reset. The queue and the history are kept (there is no sample to restart from), unlike the fault on stale stamps.
  - All-NaN IMU stamps: upstream's async wrapper compares `scan_end_time > last_imu_time` with a NaN `last_imu_time`, which is false, so it releases every frame, and the gate drops every sample. The first frame still gets its pose (it needs no IMU interval). The 5th NaN after it goes LOST, and without that the `INCOMPLETE` check (an empty queue holds the whole span) drops the frames and goes LOST past `imu_gap_reset_max`.
  - The R2 node rejects a non-finite `glim_ros.imu_time_offset` or `points_time_offset` at configure, like `wheel_odom_time_offset`.
- **Decisions**:
  - Drop, not LOST, between `max_imu_gap` and `imu_gap_reset_max`. A frame past the end of the IMU data is not yet a gap: in replay the data may still come after it (a hole at the frame), and online the frame is part of the `end_of_sequence` flush or follows a non-finite IMU stamp (a NaN `last_imu_time`), which the stamp gate handles. When more data comes, the next frame is judged by the gap policy over the whole interval since the last pose. Going LOST at once would also turn the last frames of a normal shutdown LOST. The drop does not start a gap episode, because no gap has been seen.
  - The escalation compares the hold, not the time since the last pose, with `imu_gap_reset_max`, so it is the same quantity as the gap policy uses. While frames are dropped this way the two are close: the hold grows with every frame after the last sample. A shutdown with more than `imu_gap_reset_max` (0.5 s, 5 frames at 10 Hz) of frames queued past the last IMU sample ends LOST. Online, the async wrapper holds back only frames that the IMU has not reached, so a normal shutdown leaves one or two.
  - The hold check covers (last pose, frame stamp], as the hold check on covered frames does. The rest of the scan, up to `scan_end_time`, is deskewed from the prediction without an IMU sample. That part is at most one scan long, and it is not checked.
  - The shared base class holds the change, so the CPU and GPU odometry both get it. `glim_localization_mapper` also flushes through `AsyncOdometryEstimation`, but it loads the module from `config_odometry`, which is GLIM's mapping odometry, not this one; it is not changed.
  - The R2 offset check has no ROS test. GLIM reads `config_ros.json` with nlohmann::json, which rejects `NaN`, `Infinity` and an overflowing number such as `1e999` (checked in the container), so a config file cannot reach the check. It guards a future source of the value, as the check on `wheel_odom_time_offset` does.
- **Verification** (Docker, amd64):
  - New tests in `test_replay_runner`: an uncovered frame within the limit gets a pose; one above the limit is dropped without LOST (`incomplete_drops() == 1`), and the next frame still sees the hole before it and follows the gap policy (self-reset, `hold_check_trips() == 0`, the queue and the history agree); an IMU that stops goes LOST after 5 drops; a shutdown flush through `AsyncOdometryEstimation` gives a pose for the frame 0.01 s past the last sample and drops the one 0.11 s past it, without LOST; 5 non-finite stamps in a row go LOST, 4 do not; all-NaN stamps give no pose after the first.
  - The probe oracle now judges uncovered integrations too: the random streams assert that their longest hold is at most `max_imu_gap`. The streams now have 25 uncovered integrations, up to 0.05 s (154, up to 0.2 s, before). Covered integrations: 663.
  - Each new test was checked against the code without its fix, where it can fail: without the `INCOMPLETE` check, the drop, the stop, the shutdown flush and every random-stream case fail (154 uncovered integrations again, up to 0.2 s); with the drop trimming the IMU data, the drop test fails (a hold check trip and LOST); without the non-finite LOST, the 5-stamp test fails; the all-NaN test fails only without both, as each catches it alone. The within-limit test passes without the fix, as it should: it guards against dropping too much.
  - `scripts/ci.sh` passes: core ctest 368 of 368, colcon test 208 results with no failures (`glim_localization_ros2` 23 of 23), Python tests 306 passed, replay e2e and robustness pass.
  - `ament_cpplint --linelength 100` and `ament_uncrustify` on `glim_localization_ros2`: no findings except the two provenance lines of `localization_node.cpp` (checked at 150 columns by `cpplint_derived`). On the core, which these linters do not cover (CONTRIBUTING.md), the new lines add only `whitespace/line_length` findings (1595 to 1623), in the core's existing style.
- **Review** (low-severity findings, fixed in follow-up commits):
  - A frame with a non-finite `stamp` or `scan_end_time` slipped through: with an empty queue `std::max(worst, NaN - prev)` returns `worst`, and a NaN `scan_end_time` counted as covered. Such a frame is now dropped at the top of `insert_frame`, in every `on_imu_gap` mode, before its stamp reaches the reset bookkeeping, and counted in `nonfinite_frame_drops()`. It does not go LOST, and nothing is trimmed: one corrupt message is a missing frame, the IMU and the last pose are still valid, and the next finite frame is judged by the gap policy over the whole interval since the last pose, as after a LiDAR dropout. A LiDAR that only sends such frames gives no pose, as a LiDAR that stops does. Without the fix, the new test fails both ways (a NaN stamp starts a gap episode and a self-reset; a NaN scan end gets a pose).
  - The `INCOMPLETE` proceed path skipped the 2-sample rule of the covered path: the hold limit alone allows one sample in (last pose, frame], or none when the frame interval is at most `max_imu_gap`, and upstream then predicts without an IMU factor. The rule is now applied on the real queue over (last pose, frame]; a frame that fails it is dropped like one above the hold limit (kept data, no LOST). Running `check_imu_coverage` with `scan_end := frame->stamp` was not used: the shutdown flush has no sample at the frame stamp either, so it would report `INCOMPLETE` again. Without the fix, the new test (a frame 0.03 s after the last pose with no sample since) gets a pose. The random streams now have 24 uncovered integrations (one fewer), covered still 663.
  - The wording on how `INCOMPLETE` is reached was wrong: the gate drops only stale or NaN stamps, which never advance `last_imu_time`, and the wrapper does not release frames for a stalled IMU. Online it is reached only in the `end_of_sequence` flush or with a NaN `last_imu_time`. DESIGN now says so, and also that an IMU stopping more than `imu_gap_reset_max` before the LiDAR makes the flush end LOST. The shutdown test notes that it needs a scan shorter than about 0.095 s.
  - `scripts/ci.sh` passes again: core ctest 370 of 370, colcon test 208 results with no failures, Python tests 306 passed, replay e2e and robustness pass. `ament_cpplint` and `ament_uncrustify` on `glim_localization_ros2` give the same result as above (the package did not change).
